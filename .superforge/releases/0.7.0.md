# 0.7.0 (2026-11-21)

Analytics of the app's own: what visitors view and click, and what the
server says happened, are rows in the app's own Postgres, and Superforge
Infra has no part in them. Until now `/api/visits` sent each batch on to
Infra, which decided whom the site remembers and kept the events. Now the
server decides, by the same rules, and keeps them itself. What the pages
send, and what `/api/visits` answers them, is unchanged, so
`source/frontend/visitors/` is too, but for its README.

- **Two tables**, machinery. **Schema**: `analytics_visits`, a visit a
  row (who came, from where, to which page, on what, with its `country`,
  and whether they stayed), and `analytics_events`, an event a row, a
  page's or the server's, both in `public` in
  `source/database/schema.ts`. Generate the copy's migration
  (`pnpm database:generate --name=analytics`); there's no SQL by hand.
  **They start empty: the events Infra kept before the update aren't
  moved.** Tell the owner their numbers start from the update, before it
  merges.
- **`/api/visits` keeps what it's sent**, machinery:
  `source/backend/visits/`. `record.ts`'s `recordVisit` decides whether
  the site remembers the visitor (`visitors.ts`, by `VISITOR_MODE`, their
  answer and their country), puts each event in a visit, a new one after
  30 minutes with nothing happening, and works the visit out from its
  events (`summarize.ts`: where it landed and left, the referrer, the
  campaign, the ad network, whether it bounced). `browsers.ts` drops
  bots and reads the device, with `isbot` and `bowser`. A batch is kept
  whole or not at all: a database that's down now fails it (500), and the
  page sends it again, where a batch Infra couldn't take was lost. A dev
  server keeps its own pages' events too, in the checkout's database.
  The folder's new `README.md` has the rules and how to read the tables.
- **No IP address is kept**, of anyone, where Infra kept a remembered
  visitor's: the country is read from it as the visit starts. A copy's
  privacy notice that says Superforge or Infra keeps visitors' events or
  addresses is out of date: **Owner**, to reword. `START-HERE.md`'s step
  13 says what the site keeps now.
- **A visitor's country**, machinery: `source/backend/visits/location.ts`
  downloads DB-IP's free Country Lite database (4 MB, 9 MB in memory) as
  the server starts answering, and again each week, and reads it with
  `maxmind`. It's on `context.var.locate` (`backend/context.ts`,
  `backend/index.ts`), and a test hands its own
  (`testApi({ locate })`), so a copy's `apiWith()` callers and tests that
  build the variables by hand add it. **Variable**:
  `LOCATION_DATABASE: "dbip-country-lite"` on the app's service in
  `.railway/railway.ts`, which CI applies: nothing for the owner to set.
  Without it no visitor is placed, and `ask-in-eu` asks everyone.
  `dbip-city-lite` in its place adds each visit's region and city, for
  120 MB in memory. The database is CC BY 4.0: a screen that shows where
  visitors were credits DB-IP, with `LOCATION_CREDIT` in
  `source/utilities/visitors.ts`.
- **The day's salt, in the bucket**, machinery: a visitor the site doesn't
  remember is counted by a hash made with a salt that's replaced each
  day, kept as the Storage bucket's first kind of file, `visitor_salt`
  (`source/database/storage/files.ts`), one file, `visitor-salt/today.json`.
  A copy with kinds of its own keeps them, and adds this getter and its
  place in `kinds`.
- **The server's own events**, machinery: `recordEvents(database, events)`
  in `source/backend/visits/record.ts`, in place of
  `context.var.infra.sendEvents`. An event's `user` is now `user_id`, a
  `users.id`, its `visitor` is `visitor_id`, and it has no `session`.
  `backend/auth/routes.ts` records `signed-up` with it, now with the
  visitor's ID when the site remembers them, which joins a sign-up to the
  visits that led to it. A copy's own `sendEvents` calls change the same
  way.
- **How long it's kept**, a starting point for the two numbers:
  `source/backend/visits/prune.ts` deletes events 90 days after they
  happened and visits 25 months after they started
  (`KEEP_EVENTS_FOR`, `KEEP_VISITS_FOR`, either `"forever"`), each hour,
  from a runner now listed in `backend/workflows/index.ts`'s `RUNNERS`.
- **Gone from the Infra wrapper**, machinery: `sendVisit`, `sendEvents` and
  `query`, with `source/backend/infra/events.ts`, `visits.ts` and
  `query.ts`, their answers in `answers.ts`, and their parts of
  `client.ts`, `stand-in.ts`, `choose.ts` and the README. A copy that
  calls `context.var.infra.query` for a dashboard of its own rewrites it
  as Postgres SQL over the two tables, through an operation: `->>` needs
  no parentheses there, and DuckDB's `arg_min`, `median` and
  `date_diff` have no namesakes (`summarize.ts` shows the first; the
  median is `percentile_cont(0.5) within group (order by …)`). A copy's
  tests that build an `Infra` by hand drop the three keys.
- **Sample visits**, machinery: `source/database/seed/analytics.ts`, four
  visits and their events, listed in `seed.ts`'s `SEEDERS`.
- **Packages**: `bowser`, `isbot` and `maxmind` in `package.json`.
- **Docs**: `CLAUDE.md`'s architecture list, `README.md`,
  `source/frontend/visitors/README.md`, `source/backend/infra/README.md`,
  `workbench/writing-code.md` and `workbench/commit-and-deploy.md` say
  where the analytics lives now.

How to tell a copy already has it: `source/backend/visits/record.ts`
exists, and `source/backend/infra/visits.ts` doesn't.
